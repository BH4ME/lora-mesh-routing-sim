\title{MeshEcho: Confidence-Aware Route Admission for LoRa Mesh IoT}

\author{
\IEEEauthorblockN{Zu Gao \quad Zhi Quan}
\IEEEauthorblockA{Shenzhen University, Shenzhen, China}
}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
LoRa mesh IoT networks trade source-confirmed reliability against scarce
airtime. Flooding adds redundancy but creates collisions, whereas a cached
source route can select a weak multi-hop path. We present MeshEcho, a
confidence-ranked source-routing policy with bounded route-miss recovery,
evaluated in a packet-level simulator. In 480 first-discovery unicasts over
20 held-out seeds, MeshEcho reaches 0.692 ACK completion at 93.3 s airtime,
versus 0.554/91.4 s for ETX. The seed-paired ACK difference is +0.137
(95\% CI [+0.090,+0.185]), at +1.9 s airtime. The primary policies do not
always observe the same routes: only 148/480 MeshEcho--ETX candidate sets
match. An isolated experiment with identical candidate sets on all 480
pairs retains a +0.163 ACK advantage [+0.121,+0.204], at +0.094 s per
pair. On unconditioned random pairs, flooding instead achieves 0.736 ACK
PDR/39.7 s against MeshEcho's 0.430/103.2 s. Short route TTL under block
fading also degrades MeshEcho to 0.341/71.1 s. These results support a
conditional route-selection benefit in simulation, not universal dominance
or hardware validity.
\end{abstract}

\begin{IEEEkeywords}
LoRa mesh, Internet of Things, route admission, packet delivery ratio,

\section{Introduction}
LoRa is attractive for low-power IoT because it combines long link budget
with inexpensive radios \cite{augustin2016study}. A star topology is
insufficient for obstructed or infrastructure-poor deployments, motivating
multi-hop extensions \cite{wong2024multihop,cotrim2020lorawanmesh,
centelles2021beyond}. Multi-hop forwarding is expensive: a low data rate
occupies the channel for a long time, while a flood multiplies collision
opportunities. A cached source route reduces redundancy, but one weak hop can
invalidate an otherwise useful path.

MeshEcho records lightweight link-quality evidence during discovery and
ranks candidate paths by confidence. The default confidence threshold is
zero, so an admitted route does not trigger confidence-based flooding;
bounded TTL-2 recovery occurs only when discovery finds no candidate. The
contribution is a route-selection policy evaluated against
shortest-candidate, ETX, ETT, min-hop, and flooding baselines. The
implementation is a simulation prototype, not a production mesh stack.

This paper makes four contributions:
\begin{itemize}
\item a reproducible packet-level LoRa mesh harness with time-on-air,
propagation, shadowing, half-duplex collision/capture, route discovery, and
energy accounting;
\item a quality-gated, 480-unicast first-discovery matrix over 20 held-out
seeds, with paired confidence intervals and candidate-set auditing;
\item an isolated single-flow comparison with identical observed candidate
sets, plus component and controlled route-conflict evidence; and
\item random-pair, three-hop, and fading diagnostics that expose limits of
the simulated reliability--airtime tradeoff.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}
Prior ICC LoRa studies include analytical and simulation work on coverage,
spreading-factor allocation, and frame error rates
\cite{tu2020coverage,georgiou2020coverage,amichi2019sf,hamdi2020dynamic,
afisiadis2020fer}, as well as testbed validation
\cite{to2018lorans3,rochester2020lsc}. Multihop studies expose the
flooding--airtime tradeoff \cite{wong2024multihop,centelles2021beyond} and
show that routing state and packet timing matter
\cite{sole2022implementation,pueyo2024minimalistic}. ETX is a standard
expected-transmission metric \cite{decouto2003etx}; our fixed-SF ETT row is
a sanity baseline because constant packet time-on-air makes it numerically
coincide with ETX. We therefore ask a narrower question: does confidence
admission change the reliability--airtime operating point under one shared
packet model?

\section{Protocol and Model}
\subsection{Network model}
Nodes are uniformly placed in a square and share one half-duplex LoRa
channel. Unless noted, packets have 32-byte payloads, 17 dBm transmit power,
125-kHz bandwidth, coding rate 4/5, capture threshold 6 dB, and SF7. Airtime
follows the standard LoRa symbol calculation \cite{semtech2013lora}.
Log-distance path loss with log-normal shadowing produces SNR; reception is a
logistic function of SNR margin, and overlapping packets are accepted only
when the desired signal passes capture. The simulator records collisions,
route-cache events, discovery success, and configured TX/RX energy.

\subsection{Routing policies}
Managed flooding rebroadcasts with duplicate suppression. Matched
source-routing floods a request, collects candidates arriving in a 2-s
window, and selects the shortest returned candidate. ETX and ETT use
the same discovery and forwarding machinery but minimize
\[
C_{\rm ETX}=\sum_i p_i^{-1},\qquad
C_{\rm ETT}=\sum_i {\rm ToA}_i p_i^{-1},
\]
where $p_i$ is the observed per-hop PRR. The min-hop baseline ignores link
quality after the same discovery-window configuration. MeshEcho selects the
candidate with the highest confidence, using path length only as a
tie-break. Its default low-confidence fallback
threshold is zero; only discovery with no candidate invokes TTL-2 recovery.
The ICC runs disable adaptive timeout retries. The historical
Smart-CALM firmware/simulator namespace remains in the repository but is not
an ICC protocol, ablation, or conclusion.

\subsection{Confidence update and recovery}
For each received route request, let $m_i$ be the received SNR minus the
SF-specific demodulation threshold on hop $i$, and let $I_i$ indicate a
reception marked as collided. The link score and MeshEcho's accumulated
candidate score are
\begin{align}
\ell_i &= \operatorname{clip}_{.05}^{.98}
  \left(\frac{1}{1+e^{-.7m_i}}-.18I_i\right), \\
c_1 &= \ell_1,\qquad
c_i=\operatorname{clip}_{.05}^{.98}
  \left(\min(c_{i-1},\ell_i)-.025\right),\quad i\geq2.
\end{align}
Here $\operatorname{clip}_a^b(x)=\min(b,\max(a,x))$. Thus the score
retains the weakest observed hop and charges 0.025 for each additional
hop beyond the first. It is a heuristic admission score, not an independently
calibrated end-to-end delivery probability. The destination retains every
candidate arriving before the two-second window closes; the highest score
wins, with shorter paths breaking equal-score ties. A route reply follows
the reverse selected path, and data are sent only after the source receives
that reply. Consequently, route-discovery success and ACK completion are
distinct events, both included in the reported denominator.

For a cached route created at $t_0$ and expiring at $t_e$, forwarding uses
\begin{equation}
\hat c(t)=\operatorname{clip}_0^1\!\left[
c_i-.1\operatorname{clip}_0^1\!\left(
\frac{t-t_0}{\max(1,t_e-t_0)}\right)\right].
\end{equation}
The default fallback threshold is zero,
so this age reduction never launches a low-confidence duplicate for an
admitted route. If discovery observes no candidate, the pending unicast
instead invokes a TTL-2 recovery flood. ACK-triggered cancellation applies
to delayed fallbacks, but the evaluated configuration disables timeout
retry. This precise scope matters when interpreting the near-zero
no-fallback ablation difference.

\section{Experimental Design}
\subsection{Primary matrix and metrics}
The primary matrix has 50 nodes in an $8250\,\mathrm{m}$ square, 600-s runs,
one unicast per selected directed pair (24 per seed), SF7, 4-dB shadowing,
and held-out seeds 21--40. Pairs are selected from a shared analytical graph
with edge PRR $\geq0.90$ and graph distance 2--4 hops; every selected pair
is two hops in these runs. The quality gate requires a complete pair pool,
mean graph distance at least 2, and at least 10\% of direct links below
0.99 PRR. Each pair appears once, so the estimand is first discovery rather
than repeated-pair cache reuse. Policies share node placement, flow schedule,
shadowing, reception-stream construction, discovery window, route TTL, and
RREQ relay timing. Distinct forwarding decisions still alter collision
histories and observed candidate sets. Reported paired intervals use 20
seed-level differences, not 480 independent flows.

\subsection{Generalization strata}
Separate 20-seed strata avoid pooling different estimands. Random pairs use
50 nodes in an 18-km square and mixed traffic at 4 flows/min. Deep multihop
uses 100 nodes in a 21-km square and unicast at 2 flows/min; the selection
rule permits graph distance 3--5, but all selected paths in these seeds are
three hops. Temporal-fading cases reuse four connected pairs in the 8.25-km
square with 6-dB block fading every 60 s, comparing 600-s and 30-s route
TTLs. These cases are not pooled with Table~\ref{tab:main}.

\subsection{Comparison and uncertainty procedure}
The primary comparison puts all 24 selected flows into one 600-s run for
each policy and seed. The application schedule and topology are identical
within a seed, and matched relay timing removes a known request-timing
asymmetry. Nevertheless, path choice changes reply, data, and ACK
transmissions; later discoveries can therefore see different interference
and candidate sets. This matrix estimates complete-policy behavior under
sequential operation. An audit pairs discoveries by seed, source,
destination, and request identifier, then compares exact candidate-path
sets rather than their counts alone.

To separate candidate ranking from this feedback, the isolation experiment
runs each of the same 24 directed pairs alone in a fresh 12-s simulator for
each of four policies: MeshEcho, ETX, min-hop, and matched source routing.
All runs reuse their seed's node geometry, radio settings, and reception
stream, begin the unicast at 0.1 s, share matched request relay timing,
and disable MeshEcho's route-miss fallback. No previous flow can alter a
route cache or the collision history. Candidate-set equality is verified
for each pair before interpreting selection differences. The pair pool
comes from an analytical connectivity graph, but no policy sees that graph
when discovering a route.

For a baseline $B$, let $d_s$ be MeshEcho's metric minus $B$'s metric in
topology seed $s$. In the matrix, a PDR metric is the fraction of 24
observed unicasts; in isolation it is the mean of 24 paired binary outcomes
within that seed. We report $\bar d$ and the two-sided interval
$\bar d\pm t_{.975,19}s_d/\sqrt{20}$, where $s_d$ is the sample standard
deviation of the 20 seed differences. Airtime is per 600-s run in the
matrix and per isolated pair in the ranking test. Treating 480 flows as
independent replicates would give unjustifiably narrow intervals because
pairs in one seed share geometry and shadowing.

\section{Results}
\subsection{Primary and mechanism evidence}
MeshEcho exceeds ETX/ETT by +0.137 ACK PDR (95\% paired CI
$[+0.090,+0.185]$), with +1.9 s airtime ($[+1.6,+2.3]$). Against
matched source routing it gains +0.267 ACK PDR
($[+0.208,+0.325]$) at +2.8 s airtime ($[+2.4,+3.2]$).
Destination-PDR gains also remain positive against ETX (+0.104,
$[+0.051,+0.157]$). Flooding is much cheaper in airtime (27.8 s), but
its ACK PDR is 0.444 in this conditioned matrix; its destination PDR is
0.992, underscoring the reverse-ACK distinction.

The no-confidence row is an end-to-end bundle comparison: candidate sets,
discovery success, and route length can all change. In the main matrix,
MeshEcho and ETX observe identical candidate sets on only 148/480 paired
flows, despite matched RREQ timing. Thus Table~\ref{tab:main} alone cannot
attribute the gain to route ranking. A separate isolated first-discovery
experiment restarts the simulator for each pair, disables route-miss
fallback, and obtains identical candidate sets for all 480 pairs; 447 pairs
have at least two candidates. MeshEcho gains +0.163 ACK PDR over ETX
($[+0.121,+0.204]$), at +0.094 s per pair ($[+0.078,+0.110]$).

under the static model. A controlled seven-node route-conflict audit also
finds a +0.192 ACK gain ($\pm0.100$) for the longer selected branch at
+14.3 s airtime; it is not evidence of universal superiority.

Under temporal fading, shortening TTL from 600 to 30 s increases MeshEcho's
mean route repairs from 0 to 14.5 per run, reduces ACK PDR from 0.581 to
0.341, and increases airtime from 21.8 to 71.1 s. Flooding reaches
0.561/44.7 s in the short-TTL workload. These are operating-point
boundaries, not evidence that confidence aging solves fading.

\subsection{Fairness and metric boundaries}
The matched source-route baseline uses the same discovery window and RREQ
relay timing as MeshEcho, but differing transmissions still change candidate
exposure. The isolated experiment is the stronger fixed-candidate check.
We report ACK and destination PDR separately: an ACK confirms source-side
completion, whereas destination delivery can remain high when the reverse
path fails. Airtime includes discovery, data, ACK, and recovery traffic.

\section{Discussion and Limitations}
The primary matrix shows higher ACK completion for MeshEcho than ETX at a
small airtime cost, but not a clean route-ranking effect: only 148/480
MeshEcho--ETX candidate sets match. The isolated experiment, with all
candidate sets matched, supports a conditional route-choice interpretation.
The budgeted variant falls to 0.573 ACK PDR, below default MeshEcho's
0.692; it is an unsuccessful tradeoff here, not an improvement. The
no-fallback ablation differs by only +0.002 ACK PDR in the matched matrix,
consistent with fallback having little effect in this workload. Random-pair
flooding and short-TTL fading reverse the attractive operating point, so
the result is not topology-independent dominance.

All results are packet-level simulation. The model includes path loss,
shadowing, collision timing, capture, route discovery, and energy accounting,
but omits waveform interference, oscillator offset, modem implementation
timing, duty-cycle enforcement, variable route headers, and antenna effects.
The confidence estimate and forwarding channel share the same realization in
static runs; temporal block fading is a deterministic stress model, not
hardware validation. A hardware or hardware-in-the-loop study remains
necessary for radio-specific external validity.

\subsection{Threats to interpretation}
The quality gate is a measurement contract, not a guarantee of deployment
realism. Analytical-graph pair selection removes disconnected samples but
conditions the primary estimate on usable static links. Random pairing
removes that conditioning and markedly changes the ranking; the deep stratum
tests a different scale, with actual selected graph paths all three hops.
These strata must not be averaged into one ranking.

The paired design shares topology, traffic, and reception-stream
construction, but it is not event-by-event common-random-number coupling:
different transmissions cause different collision histories. Confidence
intervals use independent seed-level differences, not individual flows.
The no-confidence ablation changes discovery as well as route selection,
and the seven-node route-conflict audit is deliberately surgical. Neither
replaces the isolated same-candidate comparison.

\subsection{Deployment boundary}
The simulated link score uses an SNR margin from successful request
receptions. It does not explicitly estimate packet loss from censored
requests, nor does it calibrate confidence against measured end-to-end
delivery. Static shadowing lets discovery and forwarding sample the same
link realization. A moving node, changing antenna orientation, or external
interferer could invalidate the ordering before the 600-s route TTL expires.
The short-TTL fading case probes one such failure mode, but its 60-s block
process is not a measured channel trace. The lower ACK rate and higher
airtime in that case show that merely rediscovering more often is not a
complete mobility solution.

A radio study should keep the selected pair schedule and two-second
discovery window fixed while logging every request candidate, chosen path,
per-hop RSSI/SNR, data arrival, reverse ACK, retry, and transmit airtime.
Repeating the isolated same-candidate test on a small SX126x or SX127x
testbed would test whether the score ordering persists under real oscillator
offset, antenna variation, modem timing, and interference. A second phase
should impose an enforceable duty-cycle budget and vary route TTL, then
report both ACK and destination PDR. Without those measurements, the present
numbers should guide simulator comparisons rather than deployment claims.

\section{Reproducibility}
Version 2.1.23 includes source, tests, seed-level CSVs, summary CSVs,
candidate-path records, and reports. The matrix, isolation, and candidate
audit utilities live in the released \texttt{tools/} directory; their exact
arguments and output prefixes are recorded alongside the raw results.
Generalization, fading, and route-conflict runners are versioned separately.
The 20 held-out seeds are 21--40; raw CSVs preserve per-seed denominators
and selected paths.
No human participants or personal data were involved, and the authors
report no external funding or conflicts of interest.

\subsection{Evidence manifest}
Raw observations are separate from interpretation. The primary CSV contains
one row per policy and seed and records 24 scheduled and observed distinct
unicasts in each row. Discovery records preserve observed candidate paths
and selected routes, allowing the 148/480 live candidate-set match to be
recomputed. The isolated CSV records each policy's result for every pair;
the report checks all 480 candidate sets and same-path outcomes. Generalization
reports keep the random-pair, deep, and fading estimands separate. Historical
2.1.22 sensitivity artifacts remain labeled as historical and are not pooled
with this holdout.

\subsection{Audit checklist}
Before interpretation, the release checks protocol coverage, paired traffic
traces, candidate exposure, route TTL, and independent reception streams.
Connected-pool runners reject incomplete pair sets and degenerate link
regimes. Temporal fading is a deterministic function of link, seed, and time
block. Reports include selection rules, route-hop diagnostics, discovery
success, and paired intervals, so high aggregate PDR cannot hide a
single-hop or discovery-limited experiment.

The raw files retain negative cases: flooding dominates MeshEcho on random
pairs, a short TTL is costly under fading, and the budgeted variant loses ACK
PDR. Fixed-SF ETT and ETX coincide because the packet airtime multiplier is
constant. CSVs, not rounded table entries, are authoritative for
recomputing intervals.

\section{Conclusion}
Across 480 one-shot unicasts, MeshEcho attains 0.692 ACK PDR/93.3 s versus
ETX's 0.554/91.4 s. The isolated same-candidate experiment supports a
route-ranking benefit, while the live matrix also changes candidate
exposure. Random-pair flooding outperforms MeshEcho, and short-TTL fading
substantially degrades it. Version 2.1.23 therefore supports a bounded
simulation result, not universal dominance or physical validation.
