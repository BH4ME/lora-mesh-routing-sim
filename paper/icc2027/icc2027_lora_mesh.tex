\title{MeshEcho-CPR: Cost-Responsive Recovery with ACK-Terminated Relay Suppression for LoRa Mesh Networks}

\author{
\IEEEauthorblockN{Zu Gao \quad Zhi Quan}
\IEEEauthorblockA{Shenzhen University, Shenzhen, China}
}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Packet loss in a LoRa mesh creates a choice between repeating a known path and
injecting another network-wide flood. Repeating too early wastes a stale route,
whereas flooding too freely multiplies airtime and collisions. We present
MeshEcho-CPR, a source and relay policy that checks the scheduled air start
before irreversible transmission commitment. A routed packet is retried once
on the same confirmed path; its recorded air start anchors the timer for at
most one deadline-feasible recovery flood. A relay records a pending flood and
cancels it only after a
physically decoded ACK passes flow, request, endpoint, reverse-path,
repair-marker, and sender checks, and only before the relay commits the
transmission.
This preserves a charged wire cost while suppressing redundant relay floods.
In a 20-seed paired evaluation with 20 nodes and 6-dB block fading, CPR reaches
0.9829 deadline ACK PDR and 0.9984 destination PDR with 6.29 s mean TX
airtime. Under the same paired seed identity, CPR also exceeds legacy MeshEcho, the
Meshtastic-like model, and the MeshCore-like model in deadline ACK PDR by
0.0351, 0.0424, and 0.0501, while using 3.94 s, 22.97 s, and 3.61 s less TX
airtime per seed, respectively. Results
are packet-level simulation evidence for this workload, not measurements of
deployed Meshtastic or MeshCore hardware. The reported implementation still
has source-return forwarding and concurrent-ACK generation defects, whose
effect must be reassessed after correction.
\end{abstract}

\begin{IEEEkeywords}
LoRa mesh, opportunistic recovery, ACK feedback, flooding suppression,

\section{Introduction}
LoRa is attractive for low-power and infrastructure-sparse deployments because
it provides a long link budget with inexpensive radios, but its low data rate
makes redundant transmissions costly
\cite{augustin2016study,bor2016scale,georgiou2017scale}. Multihop LoRa
systems therefore combine cached routes, flooding, and local forwarding
decisions \cite{wong2024multihop,cotrim2020lorawanmesh}.
We compare CPR with legacy MeshEcho's confidence-ranked routes,
Meshtastic-like delayed managed flooding, and MeshCore-like cached source
routing with explicit discovery. These behavior-level baselines expose
different costs: relay redundancy, route discovery, and recovery after a
previously confirmed path becomes unreliable.
The hard case is a route that was recently confirmed but is no longer reliable:
an immediate flood can recover delivery, yet every relay that transmits the
same recovery packet consumes airtime and increases collision exposure.

Existing route metrics such as ETX quantify expected transmissions
\cite{decouto2003etx}, but a metric alone does not specify when a recovery is
safe to start or how a relay should react to a terminal ACK that is already on
the air. This paper asks whether a protocol can use the physical ordering of
repeat, recovery, ACK, and relay-commit events to improve reliability without
paying for an unrestricted second flood.

We make three contributions:
\begin{itemize}
\item We define MeshEcho-CPR, a bounded source/recovery state machine that
  separates an initial confirmed-route attempt, one same-path repeat, and at
  most one recovery flood admitted using its scheduled start and deadline.
\item We add an ACK-terminated relay ledger. Cancellation is accepted only for
  a physically matching ACK and an uncommitted pending relay, making the
  suppression event auditable rather than an oracle-based delivery shortcut.
\item We provide a seed-paired same-condition
  comparison with legacy MeshEcho,
  Meshtastic-like flooding, and MeshCore-like source routing. All rows share
  topology, traffic, radio configuration, denominators, and seed identities;
  named products remain separate from simulator-model evidence.
\end{itemize}

The contribution is a rewritten source, recovery, and feedback core on a
preserved packet-level LoRa substrate. The substrate supplies propagation,
collision, forwarding, ACK transport, ToA, energy accounting, and evidence
ledgers so that the protocols are compared under the same physical model. We
do not claim a firmware implementation or universal superiority over every
mesh protocol.

\section{Related Work}
\paragraph{LoRa airtime, interference and feedback}
LoRa scalability depends on airtime and concurrent channel use
\cite{bor2016scale,georgiou2017scale}. Croce et al. examine imperfect
spreading-factor orthogonality \cite{croce2018orthogonality}, while
Rahmadhani and Kuipers study concurrent transmissions
\cite{rahmadhani2018concurrent}. Heusse et al. analyze reception under
multiple colliding signals \cite{heusse2023capacity}. These works motivate
explicit interference accounting; our fixed-SF7 experiment does not test
multi-SF capacity. To and Duda evaluate CSMA in a LoRa simulator
\cite{to2018lorans3}; CPR instead changes recovery and relay decisions on
a fixed shared PHY. Van den Abeele et al. show that downlink traffic can
affect confirmed-uplink delivery in LoRaWAN
\cite{vandenabeele2017scalability}. This supports accounting for ACK traffic,
without equating gateway downlinks with reverse-path mesh ACKs.

\paragraph{Multihop LoRa methods}
Surveys distinguish relay/router architectures and routing, energy and
channel-access objectives \cite{cotrim2020lorawanmesh,wong2024multihop}.
Smart-Hop combines low-overhead routing with spreading-factor-dependent
range selection to reduce data-extraction latency
\cite{ahmar2022smarthop}; MLoRa targets cost-effective coverage and balanced
energy consumption \cite{feng2022cost}. Airtime-aware approaches include
spreading-factor clustering \cite{zhu2019improving} and a minimalistic
distance-vector protocol evaluated with a time-on-air metric in
FLoRa/OMNeT++ \cite{pueyo2024minimalistic}.
Sol\'e et al. implement proactive mesh routing on ESP32 LoRa boards
\cite{sole2022implementation}. LoRaHop adds uplink/downlink mesh relaying
to LoRaWAN using concurrent transmissions and rendezvous mechanisms,
with an outdoor implementation evaluation \cite{tian2023lorahop}.
These are related approaches, not arms implemented in our experiment;
LoRaWAN extensions also differ from our peer-to-peer traffic model.

\paragraph{Routing and suppression antecedents}
DSR reuses source routes to limit overhead during stable periods
\cite{johnson1996dsr}; ETX accounts for expected transmissions and
directional delivery \cite{decouto2003etx}. Broadcast-storm analysis
motivates selective rebroadcasting \cite{ni1999broadcaststorm}.
Trickle suppresses redundant code-summary transmissions after overhearing
consistent information \cite{levis2004trickle}. ExOR coordinates
opportunistic forwarders using reception and acknowledgment information
\cite{biswas2005exor}. Thus neither overhearing nor ACK-assisted suppression
alone is new. CPR combines bounded source recovery with a matching
terminal ACK that can cancel a relay only before its commitment.

\paragraph{Product models and attribution}
The public Meshtastic algorithm description uses delayed managed flooding and
duplicate suppression; its newer direct-message path learning is outside the
managed-flood arm used here \cite{meshtasticdocs}. MeshCore documents a
path-oriented unicast mechanism that can learn a path from an initial flood and
delivery report, as well as retry and flood fallback when a path breaks
\cite{meshcore}. The present MeshCore-like arm instead isolates an explicit
RREQ/RREP/cache exchange. We therefore compare behavior-level
models of these mechanisms under one packet-level PHY; the comparison is not a
firmware clone or a hardware benchmark.

Section~\ref{sec:models} compares the mechanisms mathematically: legacy
MeshEcho ranks discovered paths, Meshtastic-like suppresses duplicate
floods, and MeshCore-like amortizes discovery through cache reuse.
Section~\ref{sec:cpr} then derives CPR's deadline admission, conditional
recovery reliability, and extra ACK cancellation opportunity. All four
policies are evaluated using the same started-transmission cost definition.
The cited literature supplies antecedents and physical modeling guidance.
The baseline timer coefficients and CPR state transitions are simulator
definitions; the conditional-probability and accounting identities below
are derived here, rather than attributed to an external algorithm.

\subsection{Common path and physical cost}
Let $P=(v_0,\ldots,v_H)$ be a loop-free source--destination path,
$D_i$ the original absolute deadline of flow $i$, and $T(q)$ the wire airtime
of packet $q$. For directional no-collision link success $q_{u\to v}^{\ell}$,
the familiar independent-hop interpretation is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
p_D(P)&\approx\prod_{h=1}^{H}q_{v_{h-1}\to v_h}^{D},\\
p_A(P)&\approx\prod_{h=1}^{H}
 q_{v_{h-1}\to v_h}^{D}q_{v_h\to v_{h-1}}^{A}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:path-reliability}
\end{equation}
The reverse ACK introduces a second directional loss opportunity.
ETX similarly incorporates forward DATA and reverse ACK delivery ratios:
its link cost is $1/(d_f d_r)$ and its path cost sums the link costs
\cite{decouto2003etx}. CPR does not implement that ETX estimator.
Equation~(\ref{eq:path-reliability}) is explanatory, not the estimator used
in the results: fading, capture, overlapping flood paths and interference
are resolved by physical packet receptions.

The modeled PHY payload includes the routing header, not just the
application bytes. With $n_P$ node IDs in a nonempty path, its byte length is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
L(q)&=10+4I_{\mathrm{disc}}+(2+2n_P)I_P
       +O(q)+I_{\mathrm{app}}L_{\mathrm{app}},\\
L_D^{(j)}(n_P)&=L_{\mathrm{app}}+12+2n_P+\zeta_j,\\
L_A^{(j)}(n_P)&=12+2n_P+\zeta_j,\qquad
 \zeta_j=\mathbf 1\{j>0\}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:wire-length}
\end{equation}
$I_{\mathrm{disc}}$ identifies RREQ/RREP, $I_P$ a nonempty encoded path,
and $I_{\mathrm{app}}$ application-bearing DATA/FLOOD. The ten-byte base
encodes kind, TTL, flow ID and endpoints; a path adds its count, index and
16-bit IDs. $O(q)$ counts every remaining present optional field, including
confidence, margin metadata, extra paths and a repair marker. The last two
lines apply to CPR DATA/FLOOD and reverse ACK with only the one-byte marker
optional: $j=0$ is initial, $j=1$ repeat and $j=2$ recovery. Routed DATA
carries the full path ($n_P=H+1$); FLOOD carries its currently accumulated
path, while its admission budget uses the maximum permitted path length.
Simulator timestamps and protocol names are not wire fields.

For PHY payload length $L(q)$, spreading factor $SF$, bandwidth $BW$,
coding-rate index $CR$, and header/CRC flags, all arms use the LoRa
symbol-count airtime expression described in \cite{augustin2016study}:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
T(q)&=\left(N_{\mathrm{pre}}+4.25+N_{\mathrm{pl}}(q)\right)
       \frac{2^{SF}}{BW},\\
K(q)&=8L(q)-4SF+28+16CRC-20IH,\\
N_{\mathrm{pl}}(q)&=8+\max\!\left\{
 \left\lceil\frac{K(q)}{4(SF-2DE)}\right\rceil(CR+4),0\right\}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:lora-toa}
\end{equation}
Here $N_{\mathrm{pre}}=8$, $CRC=1$, $IH=0$, $CR=1$ means 4/5 coding, and
$DE$ is low-data-rate optimization. Routing fields and repair markers remain
in $L(q)$; equal application payloads therefore need not have equal wire costs.
Equations~(\ref{eq:wire-length})--(\ref{eq:lora-toa}) connect route length
and recovery overhead to charged airtime. An additional byte can leave ToA
unchanged within one symbol-count bin, but is never omitted from the budget.

\subsection{Legacy MeshEcho: confidence-ranked discovery}
Let $m_h=\mathrm{SNR}_h-\gamma_{SF}$ be the received SNR margin and
$[x]_a^b=\min(b,\max(a,x))$. The legacy discovery selector uses
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
c_h&=\left[\frac{1}{1+e^{-0.7m_h}}
       -0.18\,\mathbf 1\{\mathrm{collision}_h\}\right]_{0.05}^{0.98},\\
C_0&=1,\\
C_h&=\left[\min(C_{h-1},c_h)-\lambda_H\mathbf 1\{h\geq2\}\right]_{0.05}^{0.98},\\
P_{\mathrm{ME}}^*&=\arg\max_{P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{RREQ}}}(C_H(P),-H(P)).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:meshecho-score}
\end{equation}
The incoming confidence is propagated in each RREQ. The named MeshEcho
implementation subtracts $\lambda_H=0.025$ at every extension beyond the
first hop; it does not subtract a growing $(h-1)\lambda_H$ at that extension.
Here $\mathrm{collision}_h=1$ denotes overlap during a successfully captured
reception, not a failed packet used as a score input.
The recurrence retains both the inherited penalty and the new link bottleneck.
For equal link scores $c_h=c\in[0.05,0.98]$, it reduces to
\begin{equation}
C_H=\max\{0.05,\ c-(H-1)\lambda_H\},\qquad H\geq1.
\label{eq:legacy-hop-penalty}
\end{equation}
For example, three links with score $0.9$ give $C_3=0.85$.
This additive ranking penalty differs from the multiplicative link-success
model in (\ref{eq:path-reliability}); the score is not a calibrated PDR.
The implementation also records the age-adjusted diagnostic
$S_{\mathrm{ME}}=[C_H(P)-\lambda_A a(t)]_0^1$.
In this runner the fallback-confidence threshold is zero; a route miss or
discovery timeout, rather than this diagnostic alone, triggers fallback.
CPR changes the subsequent recovery and feedback state machine, not merely
the coefficients of this selector.

\subsection{Meshtastic-like: delayed flood and duplicate suppression}
Broadcast-storm analysis identifies redundant rebroadcasting, contention
and collisions as costs of naive flooding \cite{ni1999broadcaststorm}.
MT represents managed flooding, not the newer product's full next-hop
learning behavior. A relay $v$ with SNR margin $m_v$ schedules
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
w_v&=\max\!\left\{0.05,\;0.75+
  0.6\left[\frac{8-m_v}{8}\right]_0^1+0.75U_v-b_v\right\},\\
b_v&=0.25\,\mathbf 1\{\mathrm{repeater}_v\},\qquad
U_v\sim\mathrm{Unif}(0,1),\\
t_v^{\mathrm{tx}}&=\max\{t_v^{\mathrm{rx}}+w_v,t_v^{\mathrm{queue}}\}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:meshtastic-delay}
\end{equation}
Times are in seconds. The constants are the MT runner's defaults, not
CPR's separate flood-delay parameters. A weak received margin increases the
delay. The relay request is processed at $\kappa_v=t_v^{\mathrm{rx}}+w_v$;
$t_v^{\mathrm{queue}}$ is its radio's next available time and
$t_v^{\mathrm{tx}}$ its scheduled air start. In this simulator the request
commits at $\kappa_v$, even if the radio is busy until $t_v^{\mathrm{tx}}$.
A repeated copy suppresses only a request not yet committed.
Let $e_v$ indicate an eligible pending relay, and $t_v^{\mathrm{dup}}=\infty$
if no suppressing duplicate arrives. Ignoring exact-time ties, whose order is
resolved by the event queue, the relay and physical cost are
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
z_v^{\mathrm{MT}}&=e_v\mathbf 1\{\kappa_v<t_v^{\mathrm{dup}}\},\\
A_{\mathrm{MT}}&=T_{\mathrm{src}}+
 \sum_vz_v^{\mathrm{MT}}T_v+A_{\mathrm{ACK,MT}}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:meshtastic-cost}
\end{equation}
$T_v$ is the wire cost of that relay's actual DATA copy. Only physically
started packets enter $A_{\mathrm{MT}}$. The common reporting deadline does
not add a CPR-style deadline admission rule to this baseline.
A duplicate heard after commitment cannot undo charged airtime, even before
the reserved air start. An ACK is not the suppression signal in this MT arm.

For potential loop-free flood paths $\mathcal P_F$, let $E_P^\ell$ mean that
the actual relay/reception events on $P$ produce outcome $\ell$ by $D_i$.
Flood reliability has the correlation-safe union representation
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{MT}}^\ell&=\Pr\!\left(\bigcup_{P\in\mathcal P_F}E_P^\ell\right),\\
\max_P\Pr(E_P^\ell)&\leq p_{\mathrm{MT}}^\ell
 \leq\min\!\left\{1,\sum_P\Pr(E_P^\ell)\right\}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:meshtastic-reliability}
\end{equation}
For $\ell=A$, the event includes the realized reverse ACK.
Shared relays and interference make the path events dependent, so multiplying
independent path-failure probabilities is not used to report MT's PDR.

\subsection{MeshCore-like: cache reuse and discovery}
MC isolates a source-route cache and explicit RREQ/RREP exchange.
Source-route discovery and reuse have established antecedents in DSR
\cite{johnson1996dsr}; the following cache model describes this simulator arm.
It is not a reproduction of the product's full flood-and-delivery-report
path learning. For an installed route, its cache and first-reply rules are
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\chi_{s,d}(t)&=\mathbf 1\{\text{entry exists},\ t<t_{\mathrm{expiry}}\},\\
t_{\mathrm{expiry}}&=t_{\mathrm{install}}+\mathrm{TTL},\\
P_{\mathrm{disc}}^*&=\arg\min_{P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{RREQ}}}
 t_{\mathrm{arr}}(P).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:meshcore-cache}
\end{equation}
The reported arm uses collection window $W_{\mathrm{disc}}=0$:
the destination answers the first arriving request. If no request arrives,
$P_{\mathrm{disc}}^*$ is undefined and $d=0$. DATA becomes eligible
only after the reply reaches the source. Let $d=1$ denote source receipt of
a matching RREP before discovery timeout. Its action sequence is
\begin{equation}
a_{\mathrm{MC}}=
\begin{cases}
D(P_{\mathrm{hit}}),&\chi=1,\\
Q\to S\to D(P_{\mathrm{disc}}^*),
 &\chi=0,\ d=1,\\
Q\to\mathrm{timeout},&\chi=0,\ d=0.
\end{cases}
\label{eq:meshcore-mixture}
\end{equation}
Here $Q,S,D$ denote RREQ, RREP and DATA, respectively.
Thus a miss consumes time before the routed exchange, and an unanswered
RREP still costs its started transmissions. For one isolated discovery
episode, let $A_Q,A_S$ count all started RREQ and RREP packets, including
unsuccessful replies, and $A_D$ count started routed DATA/ACK packets. Then
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
A_{\mathrm{MC}}&=\chi A_{D,\mathrm{hit}}+
 (1-\chi)(A_Q+A_S+dA_{D,\mathrm{disc}}),\\
p_{\mathrm{MC}}^\ell
 &=h\,p_{\mathrm{hit}}^\ell+
   (1-h)p_{\mathrm{disc}}p_{\mathrm{postdisc}}^\ell,
 \quad \ell\in\{A,D\}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:meshcore-cost}
\end{equation}
Here $h=\Pr(\chi=1)$, $p_{\mathrm{hit}}^\ell=\Pr(Y^\ell\mid\chi=1)$
for deadline outcome $Y^\ell$, $p_{\mathrm{disc}}=\Pr(d=1\mid\chi=0)$, and
$p_{\mathrm{postdisc}}^\ell$ is conditional on completed discovery and its
remaining deadline. This is a conditional decomposition, not an independence
assumption. Concurrent flows can share discovery; the network ledger charges
that episode once, not once for each waiting application flow.

\subsection{When cached MeshCore-like routing can cost less}
For the isolated exchange above, define
$c_H=\mathbb E[A_{D,\mathrm{hit}}\mid\chi=1]$ and
$c_M=\mathbb E[A_Q+A_S+dA_{D,\mathrm{disc}}\mid\chi=0]$.
Let $c_C$ be CPR's expected cost for the same exchange population.
Holding these conditional costs fixed gives the cache-hit break-even rule
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E[A_{\mathrm{MC}}]&=h c_H+(1-h)c_M,\\
\mathbb E[A_{\mathrm{MC}}]<c_C
 &\ \Longleftrightarrow\
 h>\frac{c_M-c_C}{c_M-c_H},\quad c_M>c_H.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:mc-cache-break-even}
\end{equation}
Subtracting $c_C$ and collecting the $h$ terms yields the second line.
Thus sufficiently frequent cheap cache hits can favor MC; avoiding discovery
alone cannot prove CPR universally better. The threshold is an analytical
condition, not an online CPR rule or a value fitted from the aggregate table.
Load and topology can change all three costs, and failed replies
remain charged in $c_M$.

\subsection{One ledger for all four arms}
For arm $X$, partition its physically started TX set into DATA, ACK, discovery,
and flood packets. The common cost is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
A_X&=A_{X,\mathrm{DATA}}+A_{X,\mathrm{ACK}}
       +A_{X,\mathrm{disc}}+A_{X,\mathrm{flood}},\\
A_{X,c}&=\sum_{q\in\mathcal T_{X,c}}T(q),\qquad
N_X=\sum_c|\mathcal T_{X,c}|.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:unified-cost}
\end{equation}
The categories are disjoint: a flooded MT DATA belongs to DATA, while a
CPR FLOOD belongs to flood. Discovery contains each RREQ/RREP once.
A queued cancellation is absent from every started-TX set; failed
transmissions are present. This separates three costs that prose alone can
hide: MT's committed relay redundancy, MC/ME discovery on a miss, and CPR's
bounded repeat/recovery work. The next section specifies when CPR may create
or remove those terms.

\section{MeshEcho-CPR}
\label{sec:cpr}
\subsection{Commit-time admission and bounded recovery}
For flow $i$, the source stores endpoints, original deadline $D_i$, selected
path $P$, route generation $g$, the initial branch, and recorded air starts.
At request processing time $\kappa$, let $t_{\mathrm{free}}(\kappa)$ be the
radio's next available time. The two event epochs satisfy
\begin{equation}
t^{\mathrm{air}}=\max\{\kappa,t_{\mathrm{free}}(\kappa)\}\geq\kappa.
\label{eq:cpr-epochs}
\end{equation}
The simulator commits the request at $\kappa$ and records its air start
$t^{\mathrm{air}}$ immediately. Let $\nu(P,g;\kappa,t^{\mathrm{air}})=1$
only if the route visible at $\kappa$ is READY, endpoint-valid, in generation
$g$, and unexpired at the scheduled start. No global PRR, future channel
sample or destination-delivery oracle is an input to the controller.

Unlike MC's RREP-installed cache, CPR installs a route only from an accepted
ACK. Write its record as $\mathcal R_d=(P_d,g_d,e_d,\phi_d)$, with expiry
$e_d$ and phase $\phi_d$. With phase abbreviations RD (READY), QU
(QUARANTINED) and NO (NO\_ROUTE), the first applicable branch updates it:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal R_d^+=
\begin{cases}
(P_A,G_s+1,t_A+\mathrm{TTL},\mathrm{RD}),&\mathcal I_{\mathrm{ACK}}=1,\\
(P_d,g_d,e_d,\mathrm{QU}),&m_d=1,\\
(P_d,g_d,e_d,\mathrm{NO}),&t\geq e_d,\\
\mathcal R_d,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\label{eq:cpr-route-lifecycle}
\end{equation}
Here $P_A,t_A$ are the accepted ACK's path and receive time, and $G_s$ is
the source-wide install counter, incremented on each installation.
$m_d=1$ only for an unacknowledged routed-initial flow at its original
deadline whose recorded generation still matches a READY route.
Stale flows cannot quarantine a newer route. A missing or non-READY route
does not trigger RREQ/RREP; CPR instead considers an initial DATA flood.

With $H$ route hops, $H_{\max}$ the flood hop bound, and marker $j$, the
CPR relay delay for received margin $m_v$ is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
w_v^{F}&=\max\!\Bigl\{0.05,\ b_F+
  0.35\left[\frac{5-m_v}{10}\right]_0^1+J_F U_v^F\\
 &\hspace{38mm}-0.15\mathbf 1\{\mathrm{repeater}_v\}\Bigr\},\\
U_v^F&\sim\mathrm{Unif}(0,1).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:cpr-relay-delay}
\end{equation}
The packet/relay context fixes the jitter draw for reproducibility.
With $b_F=0.45$ s, $J_F=0.65$ s and no repeaters, the delay is bounded by
$0.45\leq w_v^F\leq1.45$ s. It retains local margin-dependent ordering
but differs from MT's separately configured delay in
(\ref{eq:meshtastic-delay}). At most $H_{\max}-1$ forwarding waits are
reserved after the source hop, which gives the waiting-time term in the
implemented duration budgets:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
B_R^{(j)}(P)&=H\left(T_D^{(j)}(P)+T_A^{(j)}(P)\right)+\mu,\\
B_F^{(j)}&=H_{\max}\left(T_F^{(j)}+T_{A,F}^{(j)}\right)\\
 &\quad +(H_{\max}-1)(b_F+J_F+0.35)+\mu.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:cpr-bounds}
\end{equation}
For a flood, the wire lengths use the maximum permitted path.
$\mu=0.25$ s in the reported case; $0.35$ s reserves the maximum
forwarding margin penalty in (\ref{eq:cpr-relay-delay}).
The source hop has no forwarding wait. Marker $j=0$ denotes an unmarked
initial exchange, $j=1$ a same-path repeat, and $j=2$ recovery.
These are local scheduling budgets, not deterministic end-to-end latency
guarantees: contention and losses remain part of the simulation.
Truncated-ARQ research studies the delay--reliability trade-off of a finite
retry budget \cite{liu2004truncated}. CPR keeps the PHY fixed and uses the
above implementation-specific budgets to admit a repeat or recovery flood;
it does not inherit that work's modulation design or performance guarantees.

At commitment, feasibility accounts for the radio queue by using
the scheduled air start:
\begin{equation}
G_a(\kappa)=\mathbf 1\{t^{\mathrm{air}}<D_i,\ t^{\mathrm{air}}+B_a\leq D_i\}.
\label{eq:admission}
\end{equation}
A bound that fit at enqueue time may fail later; the controller evaluates
(\ref{eq:admission}) when processing the transmission request. It does not
revalidate after commitment if an ACK or route change occurs before the
reserved air start. Thus $t^{\mathrm{air}}$ is a scheduling input, not
future information about a successful reception.

Let $u,r,f$ mark committed initial, repeat and recovery transmissions with
recorded air starts,
$\iota\in\{R,F\}$ record the initial branch, and $a$ mark an accepted ACK.
At a pending initial/repeat/recovery request's commitment opportunity,
the source follows the ordered rule
\begin{equation}
a^*(\kappa)=
\begin{cases}
R_0,&u=0,\ \nu G_{R_0}=1,\\
F_0,&u=0,\ \nu G_{R_0}=0,\ G_{F_0}=1,\\
R_1,&u=1,\ \iota=R,\ r=0,\ \nu G_{R_1}=1,\\
F_2,&\iota=R,\ r=1,\ f=0,\ \nu G_{F_2}=1,\\
\varnothing,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\label{eq:cpr-time-guards}
\end{equation}
The whole rule is disabled if $a=1$ or the flow is retired.
$\varnothing$ means no new TX, not a successful delivery.
The repeat and recovery branches are evaluated only after their respective
timers create a request. Crucially, queue insertion does not set $r$ or $f$.

Here $\nu$ abbreviates $\nu(P,g;\kappa,t^{\mathrm{air}})$ and each $G$ uses
(\ref{eq:admission}). If $\tau_0,\tau_1$ are the recorded initial and repeat
air starts, the
implemented timer requests and stage limits are
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
t_{\mathrm{arm},1}&=\min\{D_i,\tau_0+B_R^{(1)}\},\\
t_{\mathrm{arm},2}&=\min\{D_i,\tau_1+B_R^{(1)}\},\\
0\leq f&\leq r\leq1,\qquad N_{\mathrm{FSM},i}=u+r+f\leq3.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:ordering}
\end{equation}
A timer at or beyond $D_i$ creates no request. A queued repeat that never
starts cannot arm recovery. Initial-flood flows have no repeat/recovery
branch and commit at most one controller injection. $N_{\mathrm{FSM},i}$
counts committed source-controller actions, not every physical source TX.
For DATA/FLOOD packets transmitted by the source radio,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
N_{\mathrm{src},i}^{\mathrm{TX}}
 &=N_{\mathrm{FSM},i}^{\mathrm{started}}+N_{\mathrm{return},i},\\
N_{\mathrm{FSM},i}^{\mathrm{started}}&\leq N_{\mathrm{FSM},i}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:source-radio-accounting}
\end{equation}
$N_{\mathrm{return},i}$ counts a returning flood copy relayed again by its
source. The evaluated implementation does not always suppress this case:
10 of 668 flows have four physical source DATA/FLOOD transmissions.
Consequently, (\ref{eq:ordering}) is not a three-TX radio bound.
All such extra packets remain in the airtime ledger.

For started transmission sets at each stage, the per-flow accounting is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
A_{\mathrm{CPR},i}
 &=I_R\!\left(A_{R_0,i}+r_iA_{R_1,i}+f_iA_{F_2,i}\right)
    +I_FA_{F_0,i},\\
A_{a,i}&=\sum_{q\in\mathcal T_{a,i}}T(q).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:cpr-flow-cost}
\end{equation}
$I_R,I_F$ identify the initial branches; both are zero for an initial
deadline drop. Each stage includes its started DATA/FLOOD and ACK packets.
No planned full-path exchange is substituted for packets that actually
failed or were cancelled.

\subsection{Reliability without independent-failure assumptions}
Measurements of bursty low-power wireless links show why successive
receptions need not be independent \cite{srinivasan2008beta}. That evidence
motivates conditioning on failure history; it is not a calibration of our
LoRa fading model.
The forwarding probabilities explain the policy but do not drive it.
For a routed-initial flow, define $E_j^\ell$ as the event that stage $j$
physically starts and produces outcome $\ell$ by $D_i$:
$\ell=A$ is a matching ACK at the source, whereas $\ell=D$ is DATA delivery
at the destination. Let
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\theta_j^\ell
 &=\Pr\!\left(E_j^\ell\;\middle|\;
       \bigcap_{m<j}(E_m^\ell)^c,\ I_R=1\right),\\
p_{\mathrm{CPR}}^\ell
 &=\pi_R\left[1-\prod_{j=0}^{2}(1-\theta_j^\ell)\right]
       +\pi_F\theta_F^\ell,\quad \ell\in\{A,D\}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:cpr-reliability}
\end{equation}
Here $\pi_R=\Pr(I_R=1)$ and $\pi_F=\Pr(I_F=1)$; initial drops supply the
remaining probability. $\theta_F^\ell$ is conditional on the initial-flood
branch. Impossible conditional branches contribute zero.
The product follows the probability chain rule, not independent retries:
each event includes the actual stage admission, route checks, channel
history and remaining time.

Expanding the routed branch isolates the incremental success from the
repeat and recovery flood:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\delta p_{R_1}^{\ell}&=\pi_R(1-\theta_0^{\ell})\theta_1^{\ell},\\
\delta p_{F_2}^{\ell}&=\pi_R(1-\theta_0^{\ell})(1-\theta_1^{\ell})
 \theta_2^{\ell}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:cpr-marginal-gain}
\end{equation}
The flood contributes only on histories not already successful for outcome
$\ell$, and its $\theta_2^{\ell}$ includes deadline rejection. These are
probability identities for the complete policy, not causal ablation effects
or claims that a repeat and a flood fail independently.

In particular, a successful DATA and failed reverse ACK can cause a repeat.
The source does not observe destination failure. Since an accepted ACK
requires a delivered DATA,
\begin{equation}
E^A\subseteq E^D,\qquad
p_{\mathrm{CPR}}^D-p_{\mathrm{CPR}}^A
 =\Pr(E^D\cap(E^A)^c).
\label{eq:cpr-destination}
\end{equation}
This accounts for correlated DATA/ACK failures without multiplying a
marginal DATA-failure probability by a marginal ACK-failure probability.
Conditioning on no accepted ACK by the reporting deadline gives
\begin{equation}
\Pr(E^D\mid(E^A)^c)
 =\frac{p_{\mathrm{CPR}}^D-p_{\mathrm{CPR}}^A}
        {1-p_{\mathrm{CPR}}^A},\qquad p_{\mathrm{CPR}}^A<1.
\label{eq:unconfirmed-delivery}
\end{equation}
The numerator follows from (\ref{eq:cpr-destination}); the denominator is
the probability of no accepted ACK. A missing ACK therefore does not imply
missing DATA. Repeating can improve confirmation while duplicating an
already delivered message. CPR does not estimate this probability online.

\subsection{ACK identity and generation}
A source accepts a physically decoded ACK only when all local identity
and lifecycle predicates pass:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal I_{\mathrm{ACK}}
 &=I_{\mathrm{id}}I_{\mathrm{endpoint}}I_{\mathrm{path}}
   I_{\mathrm{sender}}I_{\mathrm{payload}}\\
 &\quad\cdot I_{\mathrm{marker}}I_{\mathrm{start}}
       I_{\mathrm{deadline}}I_{\mathrm{generation}}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:ack-identity}
\end{equation}
The request ID must match the flow, the ACK endpoints must reverse the
application endpoints, and the stored source--destination path must be
loop-free and traversed on the correct reverse hop.
At the source, the ACK index is zero and its physical sender is $P[1]$.
The ACK has no application payload. A repeat ACK needs its repeat-start
record and original path; a recovery ACK needs its recovery-start record
and captured recovery epoch. A stale-generation ACK cannot promote a route.
The marker cannot acknowledge a merely queued attempt.
In the current implementation the generation test also gates confirmation
of the application flow. Every accepted ACK increments the shared route
generation in (\ref{eq:cpr-route-lifecycle}), even for the same path.
Consequently, one flow's accepted ACK can make another in-flight flow
with the same source and destination fail the generation test, even when
its ACK is otherwise valid. This is an implementation
defect, not evidence that the second message was undelivered.

\subsection{ACK-terminated pending relay suppression}
A relay records at most $K=8$ pending handles, keyed by relay, source,
flow, request and marker. Let $M_v$ indicate matching identifiers,
endpoints and marker, a payload-free ACK with a loop-free endpoint-consistent
path, a valid path index and physical sender, and $v$ absent from that ACK
path. This last condition keeps a required reverse-path ACK forwarder
out of the off-path cancellation rule. The relay cancels only when
\begin{equation}
c_v=p_v M_v\,
 \mathbf 1\{\tau_v^{\mathrm{ACK}}<\tau_v^{\mathrm{commit}}\}.
\label{eq:cancel}
\end{equation}
$p_v=1$ only for a still-pending, uncancelled handle.
A matching ACK must be physically decoded at $v$.
Neither a simulator-global delivery flag nor an unrelated ACK can cancel it.
Source generation validation in (\ref{eq:ack-identity}) remains distinct
from this relay rule; the evaluated Python relay ledger does not add a
generation field to its wire key.

For an eligible pending copy of airtime $T_v$, write $z_v^0$ for its
commit eligibility without this ACK cancellation, holding the local
pending-event trace fixed. Its local accounting identity is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
A_{\mathrm{relay}}&=\sum_v z_v^0(1-c_v)T_v,\\
S_{\mathrm{ACK}}&=\sum_v z_v^0c_vT_v,\qquad
\mathbb E[S_{\mathrm{ACK}}]=\sum_vT_v\Pr(z_v^0c_v=1).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:cpr-cancel-gain}
\end{equation}
Already committed TX remains charged. Bounded-ledger eviction is separately
logged and is not credited as ACK cancellation.
In (\ref{eq:cancel}), $\tau_v^{\mathrm{commit}}=\kappa_v$ is request
commitment, not its possibly later air start. MT cancels on a duplicate DATA
copy in this modeled arm. CPR retains duplicate-FLOOD suppression and
additionally cancels eligible pending copies on a matching terminal ACK.
Hearing another DATA/FLOOD copy alone does not establish destination
delivery or source confirmation.
Equation~(\ref{eq:cpr-cancel-gain}) identifies locally removed work; it is
not a proof that a different interference trajectory has lower total
network cost. Global reliability and cost require the paired experiment.

\subsection{Direct comparison with Meshtastic-like suppression}
Overhearing-based suppression and feedback-coordinated forwarding are
established mechanisms in Trickle and ExOR
\cite{levis2004trickle,biswas2005exor}. The comparison below isolates
CPR's particular terminal-ACK cancellation condition.
Fix one local pending-event trace.
Let $\mathcal D_v$ mean that a suppressing duplicate arrives before
$\kappa_v$, and $\mathcal K_v$ that a matching, physically decoded ACK
passes the relay predicates before $\kappa_v$. Let $e_v^0$ indicate a copy
otherwise eligible to commit, including ledger membership. Then
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
z_v^{\mathrm{dup}}&=e_v^0\mathbf 1\{\mathcal D_v^c\},\\
z_v^{\mathrm{dup+ACK}}
 &=e_v^0\mathbf 1\{\mathcal D_v^c\cap\mathcal K_v^c\},\\
z_v^{\mathrm{dup}}-z_v^{\mathrm{dup+ACK}}
 &=e_v^0\mathbf 1\{\mathcal D_v^c\cap\mathcal K_v\}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:cancel-signal-contrast}
\end{equation}
The last line is the additional opportunity: a terminal ACK cancels a copy
that duplicate suppression would retain. Multiplying it by $T_v$ gives the
local avoided airtime. The duplicate-only rule represents MT's cancellation
signal, whereas CPR also admits the ACK signal. These are rule evaluations
on one fixed trace, not the actual TX indicators of two complete runs:
MT and CPR use different source actions, relay timers and interference
histories. Thus the identity does not establish a network-wide cost ordering.

\section{Experimental Method}
\subsection{Workload and baseline settings}
The frozen case has 20 nodes in a $3000\,\mathrm{m}$ square, four directed
pair slots, 8 Poisson flows/min, SF7/32-byte application payloads at 17 dBm
and 125 kHz, 6-dB block fading refreshed every 60 s, a six-hop bound,
and a 30-s reporting deadline. Traffic lasts 240 s followed by a 30-s drain.
All four arms use seeds 92300--92319 and identical topology, pair pool,
scheduled application trace and radio configuration. Each paired identity
check passes; no started transmission has an incomplete RX record.
Among CPR's 656 unique deadline-confirmed flows, the recorded ACK paths
contain 624 one-hop, 17 two-hop and 15 three-hop exchanges.
Thus 95.12\% of successful confirmations are one-hop; the six-hop setting
is an upper bound, not evidence of successful four-to-six-hop operation.

Protocol-local settings are not silently treated as common parameters.
CPR and legacy ME use a 600-s route TTL and flood base/jitter
$0.45/0.65$ s. MC uses its 300-s TTL, $0.5/0.7$ s discovery-flood
base/jitter, and first-reply collection window zero.
MT uses (\ref{eq:meshtastic-delay}). No nodes have the repeater role.
These differences are part of the present behavior-level arms; matched-timer
and matched-cache sensitivity is needed to isolate the algorithm from its
configuration. The 20-seed matrix is paired workload evidence, not an
untouched validation of every product setting.

\subsection{Metrics and paired uncertainty}
Let $M_k$ be the scheduled flow count in seed $k$.
For each arm, missing ACK/delivery times are $\infty$.
The physical ledger gives
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\widehat p_{X,k}^{A}
 &=M_k^{-1}\sum_i\mathbf 1\{t_{X,i}^{\mathrm{ACK}}\leq D_i\},\\
\widehat p_{X,k}^{D}
 &=M_k^{-1}\sum_i\mathbf 1\{t_{X,i}^{\mathrm{DEL}}\leq D_i\},\\
A_{X,k}&=\sum_{q\in\mathcal T_{X,k}}T(q),\qquad
E_{X,k}=V I_{\mathrm{TX}} A_{X,k}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:ledger-metrics}
\end{equation}
All scheduled flows remain in the denominator, including failed discovery
and deadline drops. Airtime counts all network TX started within the run,
including packets that did not yield a deadline success.
TX energy uses $V=3.3$ V and $I_{\mathrm{TX}}=120$ mA; it excludes RX,
idle and sleep energy, so it is not battery-lifetime evidence.

For any seed-level metric $Y$, its paired CPR-minus-baseline interval is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\Delta_{X,k}&=Y_{\mathrm{CPR},k}-Y_{X,k},\\
\mathrm{CI}_{95}(\Delta_X)
 &=\bar\Delta_X\pm t_{0.975,19}\frac{s_{\Delta_X}}{\sqrt{20}}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:paired-ci}
\end{equation}
The seed, not each reception or relay, is the unit of replication.
Using common scenarios is motivated by simulation variance-reduction
methods \cite{glasserman1992crn}; identical workload seeds do not guarantee
beneficial coupling of every protocol event. We therefore estimate the
variance of the observed paired differences.
The intervals are nominal paired intervals; no multiple-comparison
correction or universal performance guarantee is implied.

of the legacy confidence recurrence in (\ref{eq:meshecho-score}).

\paragraph{Versus Meshtastic-like managed flooding}
The gains are 4.238 ACK-PDR points and 0.978 destination-PDR points with
78.499\% less airtime. MT already has high destination reachability;
the main trade-off improvement is fewer charged relay copies, represented
by (\ref{eq:meshtastic-cost}) and (\ref{eq:cpr-flow-cost}). Both arms
retain duplicate suppression. CPR limits controller-initiated stages and
admits the extra cancellation signal derived in
(\ref{eq:cancel-signal-contrast}); this comparison does not isolate that
signal's causal contribution from the other protocol differences.

\paragraph{Versus MeshCore-like source routing}
The gains are 5.006 ACK-PDR points and 5.333 destination-PDR points with
36.439\% less airtime. MC's miss branch in (\ref{eq:meshcore-cost}) must
complete discovery before DATA. CPR's initial DATA flood can deliver and
then confirm a reusable path, without a separate RREQ/RREP prerequisite.
Its repeat and recovery still require the remaining-time guard in
(\ref{eq:admission}); they are not guaranteed rescue attempts.

The paired ACK-PDR intervals are $[0.0192,0.0511]$ (ME),
$[0.0170,0.0677]$ (MT), and $[0.0220,0.0781]$ (MC).
These are differences in PDR, not relative percentages.
Mean started-TX counts are 81.65, 156.50, 318.25 and 153.10
for CPR, ME, MT and MC, respectively.

Fewer transmissions and shorter packets are distinct explanations for
lower airtime. Let $N_{X,k}=|\mathcal T_{X,k}|$ count started transmissions.
Define the pooled mean packet airtime
$\widetilde T_X=\sum_k A_{X,k}/\sum_k N_{X,k}$. Then
\begin{equation}
\frac{\bar A_{\mathrm{CPR}}}{\bar A_X}
 =\frac{\sum_kN_{\mathrm{CPR},k}}{\sum_kN_{X,k}}\,
   \frac{\widetilde T_{\mathrm{CPR}}}{\widetilde T_X}.
\label{eq:airtime-decomposition}
\end{equation}
The two factors separate the number of started transmissions from their
average wire cost. Against ME and MC, CPR has fewer TX but a larger mean
airtime per TX (ratios 1.178 and 1.192); against MT, both factors decrease.
They are descriptive ledger ratios; neither factor alone
establishes delivery reliability or identifies a causal protocol component.

To couple reliability to cost, let
$n_{X,k}^{\ell}=M_k\widehat p_{X,k}^{\ell}$ count successful flows, with
$\ell=A$ for source confirmation and $\ell=D$ for destination delivery.
The pooled cost is
\begin{equation}
\varepsilon_X^\ell=
 \frac{\sum_{k=1}^{20}A_{X,k}}{\sum_{k=1}^{20}n_{X,k}^{\ell}},
 \qquad \ell\in\{A,D\}.
\label{eq:delivery-cost}
\end{equation}
All failed and control transmissions remain in the numerator. CPR, ME, MT
and MC require 0.1886, 0.3192, 0.8866 and 0.3142 s per deadline delivery,
respectively. This is a pooled ratio over the existing runs, not an average
of per-seed ratios or a new experiment. It shows why destination PDR alone
does not capture MT's redundancy cost.
For source confirmation, the denominators are 656, 632, 629 and 621 ACKed
flows, respectively, out of the same 668 scheduled flows. Table~\ref{tab:holdout}
therefore charges 0.1918 s per CPR confirmation versus 0.3238, 0.9303 and
0.3187 s for ME, MT and MC. TX energy is $0.396A_X$ joules and hence supplies
no independent ranking beyond airtime.

\subsection{Connecting formulas to observed mechanisms}
The equations also specify failure regimes. If no matching ACK reaches a
relay before commitment, $c_v=0$ in (\ref{eq:cancel}) and CPR saves no
local copy through that rule. On histories where the remaining-time guard
rejects recovery, that stage contributes no success event to
(\ref{eq:cpr-marginal-gain}). Conversely,
a valid MC cache hit removes discovery cost, while MT's redundant paths can
remain useful when a route is stale. None of these definitions proves CPR
dominates either baseline for all channel histories or loads.
The MT duplicate counter is not a count of CPR terminal-ACK cancellations.

Let $H_{X,k},J_{X,k}$ count cache hits and misses,
$R_{X,k},Q_{X,k}$ successful and attempted discoveries,
$S_{X,k},U_{X,k}$ duplicate-triggered cancel flags and duplicate receptions, and
$C_{X,k}$ control TX. The observable ratios are
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
h_{X,k}&=\frac{H_{X,k}}{\max(1,H_{X,k}+J_{X,k})},\\
d_{X,k}&=\frac{R_{X,k}}{\max(1,Q_{X,k})},\qquad
s_{X,k}=\frac{S_{X,k}}{\max(1,U_{X,k})},\\
o_{X,k}&=\frac{C_{X,k}}{\max(1,N_{X,k})}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:mechanism-observables}
\end{equation}
Seed means give $(\bar h,\bar d)=(0.8647,0.9433)$ for ME and
$(0.8610,0.9550)$ for MC.
The cancel-flag-to-duplicate ratios are 0.5141 for MT and 0.1162 for CPR.
MT's counter can increment after commitment; it therefore does not measure
removed physical TX or airtime. Neither ratio estimates CPR's matching-ACK
cancellation probability. CPR's total cost follows
(\ref{eq:cpr-flow-cost}); its ACK-only savings require the matching
pre-commit events in (\ref{eq:cpr-cancel-gain}).
The four-arm aggregate cannot by itself attribute all gains to that one
component.

\subsection{Scope and limitations}
The figures describe the frozen implementation before correction of the
source-return forwarding and concurrent-ACK defects identified above.
Those defects require implementation fixes and reevaluation; changing a
formula or subtracting extra transmissions from old totals would not produce
valid corrected results. This workload also provides limited multihop
evidence because most successful confirmations use one hop.
The result does not establish superiority over current official Meshtastic
or MeshCore firmware, which contain mechanisms outside these simplified
arms. Different TTLs, discovery windows, forwarding delays and wire headers
can affect the outcome. Matched-configuration sensitivity, a same-wire
no-cancellation ablation, additional loads and route depths, and measured
radio-channel or over-the-air validation are needed for stronger causal
and deployment claims. The simulator irrevocably reserves TX at request
processing, so a later ACK cannot cancel a reserved but not yet on-air
packet; a hardware queue may expose a different cancellation window.
Compilation of a separate firmware prototype is
not a hardware PDR experiment.

\section{Conclusion}
MeshEcho-CPR couples scheduled-start deadline admission, one same-path repeat,
at most one recovery flood, and matching-ACK suppression of pending relays.
The equations specify what local information admits each action, what an ACK
can terminate, and which transmissions must still be charged.
The frozen 20-seed runs show higher deadline confirmation and lower TX
airtime than the legacy MeshEcho, Meshtastic-like and MeshCore-like models.
These comparisons remain provisional pending correction and reevaluation
of the audited forwarding and ACK-generation defects.
