\title{MeshEcho: Confidence-Aware Route Selection for LoRa Mesh IoT}

\author{
\IEEEauthorblockN{Zu Gao \quad Zhi Quan}
\IEEEauthorblockA{Shenzhen University, Shenzhen, China}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
LoRa mesh IoT networks trade source-confirmed reliability against scarce
airtime. A cached source route can remain weak when the channel changes.
We compare MeshEcho's original SNR-margin heuristic with one frozen
correction that ranks paths by their weakest model-inferred hop PRR, without
an extra hop penalty. In a packet-level simulator, 20 held-out topology
seeds (51--70) yield 793 repeated-pair unicasts per policy under 6-dB
block fading. The calibrated variant attains 0.624 ACK PDR versus 0.509
for old MeshEcho: a seed-paired +0.115 (95\% CI [+0.031,+0.200]) at
+2.47 s airtime and +5.09 J modeled energy per run. A PRR-product
comparator with matched route-miss fallback attains 0.621 ACK PDR; the
calibrated-minus-comparator interval crosses zero. Static-channel and
isolated-first-discovery ACK gains over old MeshEcho are also uncertain;
the latter has identical candidate sets for all 480 pairs. Reducing route
TTL from 600 to 30 s under fading lowers calibrated ACK PDR by 0.304
and adds 41.05 s airtime. The result supports a bounded simulation
correction, not strong-baseline superiority or hardware validity.
\end{abstract}

\begin{IEEEkeywords}
LoRa mesh, Internet of Things, route selection, fading, packet delivery ratio,
airtime
\end{IEEEkeywords}

\section{Introduction}
LoRa is attractive for low-power IoT because it combines long link budget
with inexpensive radios \cite{augustin2016study}. A star topology is
insufficient for obstructed or infrastructure-poor deployments, motivating
multi-hop extensions \cite{wong2024multihop,cotrim2020lorawanmesh,
centelles2021beyond}. Multi-hop forwarding is expensive: a low data rate
occupies the channel for a long time, while a flood multiplies collision
opportunities. A cached source route reduces redundancy, but one weak hop can
invalidate an otherwise useful path.

MeshEcho records link observations during discovery and ranks candidate
source routes. Its original score clips an SNR-margin heuristic and penalizes
each additional hop. That combination can over-select a direct path that is
temporarily favorable when the route remains cached across channel changes.
We test one frozen correction: rank by the weakest model-inferred hop PRR
from a received route request (RREQ), without a separate hop penalty. The
default confidence threshold is zero, so a selected route does not trigger
confidence-based flooding; bounded TTL-2 recovery occurs only when
discovery finds no candidate. This is a simulation policy study, not a
production mesh stack or an experimentally calibrated radio model.

This paper makes four contributions:
\begin{itemize}
\item a reproducible packet-level LoRa mesh harness with time-on-air,
propagation, shadowing, half-duplex collision/capture, route discovery, and
energy accounting;
\item a prespecified 20-seed repeated-pair fading holdout, with matched
PRR-product and route-miss-recovery controls and seed-paired intervals;
\item an isolated 480-pair first-discovery comparison that fixes candidate
exposure, plus a static-channel control; and
\item short-TTL and ACK-feedback diagnostics that expose the airtime and
energy costs of attempted stale-route recovery.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}
Prior ICC LoRa studies include analytical and simulation work on coverage,
spreading-factor allocation, and frame error rates
\cite{tu2020coverage,georgiou2020coverage,amichi2019sf,hamdi2020dynamic,
afisiadis2020fer}, as well as testbed validation
\cite{to2018lorans3,rochester2020lsc}. Multihop studies expose the
flooding--airtime tradeoff \cite{wong2024multihop,centelles2021beyond} and
show that routing state and packet timing matter
\cite{sole2022implementation,pueyo2024minimalistic}. ETX is a standard
expected-transmission metric \cite{decouto2003etx}; in our fixed-SF setting
ETT ranks paths identically to ETX because packet time-on-air is constant.
The stronger comparator here is the product of model-inferred hop PRRs, with
a matched route-miss fallback control. We ask whether correcting MeshEcho's
heuristic improves ACK-confirmed delivery under fading without claiming
superiority over that PRR-product baseline.

\section{Protocol and Model}
\subsection{Network model}
Nodes are uniformly placed in a square and share one half-duplex LoRa
channel. Unless noted, packets have 32-byte payloads, 17 dBm transmit power,
125-kHz bandwidth, coding rate 4/5, capture threshold 6 dB, and SF7. Airtime
follows the standard LoRa symbol calculation \cite{semtech2013lora}.
Log-distance path loss with log-normal shadowing produces SNR; reception is a
logistic function of SNR margin, and overlapping packets are accepted only
when the desired signal passes capture. The simulator records collisions,
route-cache events, discovery success, and configured TX/RX energy.

\subsection{Routing policies}
Managed flooding rebroadcasts with duplicate suppression. Matched
source-routing floods a request, collects candidates arriving in a 2-s
window, and selects the shortest returned candidate. ETX and ETT minimize
\[
C_{\rm ETX}=\sum_i p_i^{-1},\qquad
C_{\rm ETT}=\sum_i {\rm ToA}_i p_i^{-1},
\]
where $p_i$ is the simulator-predicted PRR from the SINR of a successfully
received RREQ on hop $i$, not a PRR measured from repeated packets. The
min-hop baseline first minimizes hop count, then breaks ties by higher
confidence score and lexicographic path order. Original MeshEcho selects
the candidate with highest heuristic confidence; its calibrated variant
maximizes the weakest modeled hop PRR. PRR-product maximizes the product
of those same modeled PRRs. A matched PRR-product control also enables
MeshEcho's TTL-2 route-miss fallback; the plain PRR-product control does
not. All routed policies use the same 2-s discovery window, 600-s route
TTL unless stated, and no adaptive timeout retry. The historical
Smart-CALM firmware/simulator namespace remains in the repository but is not
an ICC protocol, ablation, or conclusion.

\subsection{Confidence update and recovery}
For each received route request, let $m_i$ be the received SNR minus the
SF-specific demodulation threshold on hop $i$, and let $I_i$ indicate a
reception marked as collided. The link score and MeshEcho's accumulated
candidate score are
\begin{align}
\ell_i &= \operatorname{clip}_{.05}^{.98}
  \left(\frac{1}{1+e^{-.7m_i}}-.18I_i\right), \\
c_1 &= \ell_1,\qquad
c_i=\operatorname{clip}_{.05}^{.98}
  \left(\min(c_{i-1},\ell_i)-.025\right),\quad i\geq2.
\end{align}
Here $\operatorname{clip}_a^b(x)=\min(b,\max(a,x))$. The old score retains
the weakest observed hop and charges 0.025 for each additional hop beyond
the first. It is a heuristic, not an independently calibrated delivery
probability. The frozen new variant uses the simulator's same RREQ SINR-to-PRR
mapping as ETX, but no 0.98 saturation or additional hop penalty:
\begin{equation}
\hat p_i=f_{\rm sim}(\mathrm{SINR}_i),\quad
C_{\rm cal}=\min_i\hat p_i,\quad
C_{\rm prod}=\prod_i\hat p_i.
\end{equation}
Both scores use only successfully received RREQs; failed or censored
requests do not supply an empirical PRR estimate. The destination retains every
candidate arriving before the two-second window closes; the highest score
wins, with shorter paths breaking equal-score ties. A route reply follows
the reverse selected path, and data are sent only after the source receives
that reply. Consequently, route-discovery success and ACK completion are
distinct events, both included in the reported denominator.

For a cached route created at $t_0$ and expiring at $t_e$, forwarding uses
\begin{equation}
\hat c(t)=\operatorname{clip}_0^1\!\left[
c_i-.1\operatorname{clip}_0^1\!\left(
\frac{t-t_0}{\max(1,t_e-t_0)}\right)\right].
\end{equation}
The default fallback threshold is zero,
so this age reduction never launches a low-confidence duplicate for a
selected route. If discovery observes no candidate, the pending unicast
instead invokes a TTL-2 recovery flood. ACK-triggered cancellation applies
to delayed fallbacks, but the evaluated configuration disables timeout
retry. An optional ACK-guard variant evicts the same cached route after an
unconfirmed source DATA transmission; it never retransmits that flow and
cannot use future-channel knowledge. It is a diagnostic, not the proposed
default.

\section{Experimental Design}
\subsection{Frozen split and repeated-pair workload}
Seeds 41--50 were a development set for one score correction. After fixing
the score, fallback controls, code, and comparisons, we ran untouched seeds
51--70 once as the holdout. The primary workload has 50 nodes in an
$8250\,\mathrm{m}$ square, 600-s runs, four repeatedly selected directed
pairs, SF7, 4-dB static shadowing, and 6-dB block fading every 60 s.
Application unicasts follow a shared 4-flows/min Poisson trace; there are
793 scheduled and observed unicasts per policy across the 20 holdout seeds.
No broadcast flows are scheduled. A shared analytical graph selects four
pairs per seed with edge PRR $\geq0.90$ and graph distance 2--4 hops; the
graph is never supplied to a routing policy. Every pair recurs in at least
two fading blocks. The quality gate requires a complete pair pool, mean
graph distance at least two, and at least 10\% of direct links below 0.99
static PRR. These are conditioned repeated-pair estimates, not general
random-pair performance.

\subsection{Controls, candidate exposure, and uncertainty}
The static control retains the same seeds, pairs, and application traces but
sets temporal fading to zero. The short-TTL control retains 6-dB fading and
reduces route TTL from 600 to 30 s. An optional 15-s ACK-guard variant is
tested under the same traces; a later confirmed ACK cancels older guards for
that exact route. All policies share the same topology, flow schedule,
reception-stream construction, discovery window, and matched RREQ relay
timing. Different forwarding decisions still change interference, so live
candidate sets need not match. We compare their recorded path sets exactly.

The isolation check uses a fresh 12-s simulator for each of 24 directed
pairs in each holdout seed and each of six policies: old MeshEcho, calibrated
MeshEcho, ETX, PRR-product, min-hop, and matched source routing. It starts
one unicast at 0.1 s, disables route-miss fallback, and uses the same node
geometry and reception-stream construction. No preceding flow can alter a
cache or collision history. The pair pool is graph-conditioned, but no
policy receives that graph. The 2.1.23 one-shot seeds 21--40 are historical
evidence and are not reused as a fresh holdout.

ACK PDR requires a source-received reverse-path ACK; destination PDR only
requires DATA arrival. Each protocol's seed-level PDR divides its ACK or
destination count by that seed's observed unicasts; the reported mean gives
each of the 20 seeds equal weight. For a policy difference $d_s$, we report
$\bar d\pm t_{.975,19}s_d/\sqrt{20}$, pairing by seed. Isolation first
averages the 24 pair outcomes within each seed. Airtime and modeled TX/RX
energy are per 600-s run, except isolation airtime, which is per pair.
Flows within a seed are not independent statistical replicates.

\section{Results}
\subsection{Repeated-pair holdout}
Table~\ref{tab:main} reports seed means on the frozen 51--70 split. Under
fading, the calibrated score increases ACK PDR from 0.509 to 0.624 relative
to old MeshEcho, while consuming 2.5 s more airtime and 5.1 J more modeled
energy per seed. It does not separate from the matched-fallback PRR-product
control, which reaches 0.621 ACK PDR at 23.8 s airtime. The plain
PRR-product row reaches 0.627/23.8 s but has a different route-miss
recovery budget. Thus the old-score correction is supported; a new
strong-baseline advantage is not.

\subsection{Controls and adverse results}
Under the static control, calibrated and old MeshEcho reach 0.717 and 0.716
ACK PDR, a paired +0.000 difference ($[-0.074,+0.075]$). Calibrated
airtime rises +1.22 s ($[+0.09,+2.34]$), and modeled energy rises
+2.50 J ($[+0.19,+4.82]$). The fading improvement therefore does not
generalize as a clear static gain. Under the 30-s TTL fading control,
calibrated reaches 0.320 ACK PDR at 64.8 s airtime and 126.4 J energy.
Against its own 600-s TTL result on identical seed-level traces, the paired
ACK change is $-0.304$ ($[-0.393,-0.215]$), airtime changes by +41.05 s
($[+31.40,+50.70]$), and energy by +79.25 J
($[+60.50,+98.01]$). Route discoveries rise by 12.45 per seed. This is a
system-level TTL comparison, not an isolated cache mechanism effect.

ACK-guard eviction also fails as a recovery strategy: in the fading holdout,
old MeshEcho's ACK PDR falls from 0.509 to 0.471 while airtime rises from
21.2 to 41.1 s; PRR-product falls from 0.627 to 0.526 while airtime rises
from 23.8 to 33.7 s. The fixed implementation cancels older pending guards
when a later flow on the same route is acknowledged. It cannot infer from
an absent ACK whether the DATA failed, the reverse ACK failed, or the path
later recovered. These rows are negative diagnostics, not a proposed default.

Table~\ref{tab:diagnostics} reports descriptive fading diagnostics omitted
from the delivery table. Calibrated MeshEcho and PRR+fb both attempt 4.15
discoveries per seed; their mean ACK delays are 0.540 and 0.541 s among
ACKed flows. ACK eviction raises discovery attempts, collision rate, and
conditional delay while reducing cache hits. The legacy repair counter is
0.15 per seed for each non-eviction policy, but mixes cache expiry and failed
discovery; it does not count successful route repairs.

\section{Discussion and Limitations}
The calibrated score corrects a fading weakness of the original heuristic
in the frozen repeated-pair workload, but it is a familiar bottleneck PRR
metric, not evidence of a new routing optimum. Its ACK result is
statistically indistinguishable from matched-fallback PRR-product, and its
static and fixed-candidate ACK gains over old MeshEcho are uncertain. The
fading cost is positive in airtime and modeled energy. The negative ACK-guard
and short-TTL results rule out a simple stale-route repair claim.

All results are packet-level simulation. The model includes path loss,
shadowing, collision timing, capture, route discovery, and energy accounting,
but omits waveform interference, oscillator offset, modem implementation
timing, duty-cycle enforcement, variable route headers, and antenna effects.
The confidence estimate and forwarding channel share the same realization in
static runs; temporal block fading is a deterministic stress model, not
hardware validation. A hardware or hardware-in-the-loop study remains
necessary for radio-specific external validity.

\subsection{Threats to interpretation}
The quality gate is a measurement contract, not a guarantee of deployment
realism. Graph-qualified pair selection excludes disconnected sources and
conditions the estimate on usable static links; the four-pair trace also
favors cache reuse. The old-score random-pair result shows why these strata
must not be pooled. Candidate exposure is identical in isolation, but
live repeated-pair policies diverge after their transmissions change later
collisions. Shared seeds and reception-stream construction are not
event-by-event common random numbers. All intervals use 20 topology seeds,
not 793 or 480 independent packets. The 30-s TTL comparison changes the
whole subsequent discovery and interference history, so it does not isolate
one cache-event mechanism.

\subsection{Deployment boundary}
The modeled hop PRR comes from the SINR of a successful RREQ. It does not
account for censored requests or calibrate itself against measured packet
loss. Static shadowing lets discovery and forwarding share a link
realization; the 60-s block-fading process is a synthetic stressor, not a
measured channel trace. Moving nodes, antenna changes, duty-cycle limits,
and external interferers may reorder routes before a 600-s TTL expires.
The higher rediscovery cost with 30-s TTL shows that more frequent route
selection alone is not a mobility solution.

A radio or hardware-in-the-loop study should preserve the pair schedule and
two-second discovery window while logging each request candidate, selected
path, per-hop RSSI/SNR, DATA arrival, reverse ACK, and airtime. An SX126x or
SX127x testbed with enforceable duty-cycle limits could determine whether
the score ordering persists under real timing and interference. Until then,
the results are simulator comparisons, not deployment guarantees.

\section{Reproducibility}
Version 2.1.24 includes source, tests, seed-level CSVs, summary CSVs,
candidate-path records, and reproduction commands in the holdout 51--70
result group. The
repeated-pair cases use 51--70; isolated rows use the same topology seeds
with 24 fresh pairs per seed. Raw CSVs preserve each seed's denominator,
application-trace hash, discovery candidate paths, and selected routes.
The 30-s TTL is specified by the case configuration and report header;
the raw CSV identifies the case but has no separate numeric TTL column.
Earlier 21--40 data remain versioned as 2.1.23 history and are not pooled
with the holdout. No human participants or personal data are involved.
AI-assisted drafting and code review were used; the numerical claims here
were independently recomputed from raw CSVs. Final disclosure and author
metadata remain the submitting authors' responsibility.

\section{Conclusion}
Across 20 frozen fading seeds and 793 repeated-pair unicasts per policy,
calibrated MeshEcho improves ACK PDR over the old score by +0.115
($[+0.031,+0.200]$) at +2.47 s airtime, but does not outperform a
matched-fallback PRR-product baseline. Static and isolated ACK intervals
cross zero; shortening TTL sharply reduces reliability and raises cost.
The supported contribution is a reproducible, bounded score correction and
honest comparison under a packet-level model, not universal routing
superiority or physical validation.
