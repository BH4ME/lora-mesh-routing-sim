# ICC 2027 Comparison: MeshEcho 2.1.2

This report summarizes the refreshed four-scenario ICC matrix generated by
`tools/run_icc_experiments.sh` with
`OUT_PREFIX=meshecho_v2_1_2`. The raw files and long-format summaries are
stored under `results/meshecho_v2_1_2_50n_*`.

Each scenario uses 50 nodes in a 3000 m square, 1200 s of simulated time, eight
fixed unicast pairs, SF9/BW125 kHz/CR 4/5, 17 dBm transmit power, and 20 seeds.
The high-shadowing scenario uses 6 dB shadowing instead of 4 dB; the high-load
scenario uses 10 messages/min instead of 6 messages/min.

`unicast_pdr` is source-side ACK-confirmed delivery. The separate
`destination_unicast_pdr` metric records DATA arrival before the ACK returns.
All values below are means over the 20 seeds. The complete 37-metric summaries
also include standard deviations and two-sided 95% Student-t intervals.

## Interpretation

- Smart-CALM has the highest ACK-confirmed PDR among the evaluated methods in
  repeated unicast, mixed traffic, and stronger shadowing, while using much
  less airtime than managed flooding.
- Under high offered load, Smart-CALM retains a substantial reliability
  advantage over CALM and MeshCore-like, but its airtime and tail ACK delay
  increase.
- `smart-calm-no-fallback` confirms that removing bounded rescue forwarding
  reduces reliability.
- `smart-calm-static` usually spends more airtime and fallback forwarding than
  the adaptive method, although the mixed and shadowing PDR means are close.
- `smart-calm-no-confidence` is identical to Smart-CALM in the first three
  scenarios because the sampled topologies rarely create a route-length versus
  confidence conflict. This ablation should not be used to claim that
  confidence ranking is irrelevant.

The appropriate paper claim is therefore a reliability-airtime-delay tradeoff,
not dominance on every metric. This packet-level model also does not replace
waveform-level LoRa validation or board-level current measurements.
